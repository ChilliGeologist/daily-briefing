Which way it leans

Three ways to draw it · on a real story from 16 October

Each option shows the same story with the same judgements. The full panel sits inside the article, under the summary; under it is the small version for the front page. Tap any dot, bar or row to see the words that put an outlet there.

The judgements here are the real ones the current model made. Six of the eight outlets were judged; the BBC and AP had no quote for Trump that passed the checks, so they're left off. SMH and The Age share one story, so they sit together.

Option A

The scale

One line per person or party, from "against" through "plain" to "for". Each outlet is a dot placed where its words put it. Outlets that agree bunch together, so a split shows at a glance.

The outlet table

Your pick

Which one?

  1. A, the scale, is my recommendation. It's the most "graphic" of the three, a disagreement jumps out as dots on opposite sides, and it stays readable on a phone.
  2. B, the split bar, is the simplest, but it hides which outlet is which until you tap.
  3. C, the table, shows everything, including each outlet's headline, but it's the least like a picture.

Mixing is fine too: the scale inside the article with the split bar on the front page, say. Colours, sizes and labels are easy to change after you pick.
